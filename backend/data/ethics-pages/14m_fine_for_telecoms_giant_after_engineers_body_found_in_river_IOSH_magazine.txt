£1.4m fine for telecoms giant after engineer's body found in river

                  Open-access content
              

Tuesday 11th June 2024

From the archive:  Just so you know, this article is more than 1 year old.

Family handout

The UK telecommunications giant Openreach, owned by BT, has been fined £1.34 million for breaching health and safety laws for staff working near water after an engineer died whilst trying to repair a telephone line.Alun Owen had been sent to work on a customer's telephone line which ran across the river near Bangor, north Wales, in October 2020.The village had been flooded after the River Aber burst its banks following heavy rain, which meant the river was much higher and faster flowing than usual.The 32-year-old entered the water and made his way to an island in the middle of the river to try and throw a new telephone cable across to the other side by taping it to a hammer and then throwing it. Whilst attempting to cross the remaining section of the river, he slipped in a deeper part and the force of the river swept him away.An investigation by the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) and North Wales Police revealed there was no safe system of work in place for work on or near water, nor had Alun – and others working by the river – received training, information or instruction on safe working on or near water.Companies should learn the lessons from this incident if they have staff who may work on or near waterEarlier this month, Openreach pleaded guilty to breaching section 2(1) of the Health and Safety at work Act at Llandudno Magistrates' Court. As well as the £1.34 million fine, Openreach  – owned by BT which turns over around £5 billion – was ordered to pay prosecution costs of £15,858.Describing it as a 'protracted and lengthy' investigation, North Wales Police Detective Chief Inspector Andy Gibson said it was critical that any failings were identified and acted upon.HSE inspector Christina Roberts explained that Alun's death would have been preventable had an effective system for working on or near water been in place and should not have been put in the unsafe working situation.'Companies should learn the lessons from this incident if they have staff who may work on or near water and be aware that HSE will not hesitate to take appropriate enforcement action against those that fall below the required standards,” she warned.Judge fines BT record £90k for ‘unacceptably slow response’ to dangerous streetworks warningsBT engineer sustained broken back in 7 m fall
