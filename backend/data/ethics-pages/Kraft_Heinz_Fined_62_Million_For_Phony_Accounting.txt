ASSOCIATED PRESSKey FactsThe company best known for its ketchup, canned beans and macaroni and cheese manipulated its accounting to make it seem like it was making its products more cheaply than it really had been.That inflated its earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), a key financial metric watched by investors. The company reversed $208 million in the purported cost savings, racked up from 2015 through 2018, after the SEC began investigating the company.Kraft Heinz violated anti-fraud and recordkeeping requirements in federal securities laws, the SEC said, but the company did not admit or deny wrongdoing in the settlement.Its shares fell 0.36%, to $35.94, in trading Friday morning. A spokesperson for Kraft Heinz told Forbes that the company “fully cooperated” with the SEC’s investigation and that it has fixed its bookkeeping processes to prevent future manipulation.Crucial Quote“No matter how complex and far-reaching the financial misconduct, we will vigorously pursue wrongdoers because that’s what investor protection requires,” said Gurbir Grewal, head of the SEC’s enforcement division and until July the attorney general of New Jersey.Read MoreGot a tip?
