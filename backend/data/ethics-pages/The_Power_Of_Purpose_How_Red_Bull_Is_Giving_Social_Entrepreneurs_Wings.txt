 Red Bull Amaphiko, South Africa Red Bull

Red Bull has always been known as one of the most innovative marketers on the planet: ranging from their pioneering work on proprietary experiential platforms like Red Bull Air Race and the hyper-credible Red Bull Music Academy, to their world-class content production engine Red Bull Media House, to frankly jaw-dropping moments like Red Bull Stratos, which featured Felix Baumgartner jumping from space. The brand has always been ahead of the curve when it comes to understanding what societal expectations are, so it comes as no surprise that it has two well-rounded platforms that support social entrepreneurs–Red Bull Amaphiko (aimed at social entrepreneurs from all backgrounds) and Red Bull Basement (aimed at social entrepreneurs coming more from a technology and coding background). Both programs are rooted in Red Bull's purpose which is articulated as "to give wings to people and ideas," and are different from the companies CSR initiatives which are focused on the Wings for Life World Run Foundation which works on the mission to find a cure for spinal cord injury.
Red Bull Amaphiko (pronounced "amapico" which is the Zulu word for "wings") originated with the Red Bull South Africa team, who developed the platform to help South African entrepreneurs. The program was so successful that Red Bull has now expanded it into multiple markets like Brazil and the United States. So far, 110 social entrepreneurs have been through the Red Bull Amaphiko Academy programs in these countries, with over 70% running and growing their businesses with sustainable revenue streams and strong social impact. They look for early stage, grassroots social entrepreneurs tackling topics in areas such as education, employment, environment, sustainability, or health and wellness, and provide them with development programs and tools, from the main Amaphiko Academy (which includes 10 day residential and 18 month development program) to weekend boot camps, digital know-how and resources for social entrepreneurs.

One of the graduates of the Amaphiko program is Marisa Hamamoto, the founder of the Infinite Flow Dance Company, an inclusive dance company billed as "America's 1st professional wheelchair ballroom dance company," featuring dancers with and without disabilities. The goal of Infinite Flow is to use dance to inspire inclusion, and her company has performed all around the world. I asked Hamamoto about her experience with the Amaphiko program. "Red Bull Amaphiko took me through a life-changing journey where I confronted a past I was in denial for 15 years: beyond a stroke survivor, I was raped during my teens by a ballet coach who did not believe in me.  During the 18 month fellowship, I worked through these emotions, and it was not easy, but I am finally free and was able to forgive.  Interestingly, my business thrived thereafter. One thing I learned through Red Bull Amaphiko journey: Your cup must be filled to fill the cup of others. Leadership development is not just about learning how to strategize, build teams, fundraise, etc., but also to remove barriers that exist in the leader themselves."

 Red Bull Amaphiko graduate, Marisa Hamamoto, Founder of the Infinite Flow Dance Company Red Bull

Red Bull Basement is the other social impact program, which focuses more on helping hackers, coders, and makers–any kind of creatives who are using technology to solve social and environmental challenges, to help bring their ideas to fruition. Launched in São Paulo, Brazil, Red Bull Basement has expanded to more than 30 countries since 2015, under the uniting principle of #techforgood, and will expand this year to 30 countries including the U.S.A, U.K., South Africa, Turkey, Spain, and Switzerland. In addition to mentorship, networks, and community, the participants also get access to an innovation lab with state of the art technology tools (depending on the country and activity).

For instance, Pluvi.On is an early warning system for flooding utilizing "pluviometers" which were created during the two-month Red Bull Basement Residency by two socially minded engineers. They wanted to lessen the destruction caused by extreme flooding in Brazil. By using a network of sensors and machine learning, the company is able to give advance warning of flooding by alerting local authorities and allowing residents to make decisions crucial to their safety. Some of the other projects that Red Bull Basement has supported include an open source platform to promote accessibility, an affordable coding school for disadvantaged youth, a sensor that can save water in domestic showers, gloves used as interfaces to guide people with visual impairment in the city.

 Red Bull Basement, Sao Paulo, Brazil Red Bull

Red Bull Basement and Red Bull Amaphiko are excellent examples of how a brand should approach social purpose. First, ensure that it is in sync with the overarching purpose of the brand - in this case, to "give wings to people and ideas." Secondly, by starting small and scaling in a thoughtful, iterative manner, working with local market teams to identify best practice and to test and refine the proposition as it grows to new markets. Thirdly, by ensuring that the participants are supported not only financially but holistically with mentorship, coaching, storytelling skills and more. As the world moves in a direction where "doing good is the new cool," it is a testimony to Red Bull's foresight that it has found innovative ways to move beyond its core focus on sports and culture into a powerful new social movement that Millennials and Gen-Z consumers are eager for, future-proofing their cultural credibility for decades to come.
