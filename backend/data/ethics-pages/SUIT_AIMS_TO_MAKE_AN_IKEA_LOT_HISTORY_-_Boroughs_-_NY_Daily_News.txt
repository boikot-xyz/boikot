        SUIT AIMS TO MAKE AN IKEA LOT HISTORY
        
    

        
        Wednesday, November 22nd 2006, 12:00 AM
    

Print
Email
Suggest a Story

THE MUNICIPAL ART Society is suing Ikea to stop the conversion of a historic boat basin on the Red Hook waterfront to a parking lot for the upcoming furniture megastore.
Preservationists are concerned the so-called Graving Dock No. 1, constructed in 1865 for dry dock ship repairs, will disappear forever if the planned demolition and construction go forward.
The cost, they say, will be counted not just in lost maritime jobs and services but also in the disappearance of an important waterfront landmark.
The suit, which was filed in Brooklyn Federal Court, also names the Army Corps of Engineers. Kent Barwick, president of the Municipal Arts Society, said the corps had unlawfully granted a permit for the construction without input from civic groups.
"We are left with no alternative but legal action," Barwick said. "The law requires a proper historic review, and the public deserves it."
Ikea spokesman Joseph Roth responded that the lawsuit had no merit, saying that the project - which calls for a 22-acre store set to open next year - passed comprehensive reviews and has the support of community leaders.
"The lawsuit filed recently by the Municipal Art Society is an unfortunate attempt to delay a project that will create hundreds of new, high-quality jobs for Brooklyn residents and will return the historically inaccessible waterfront to the neighborhood community," Roth said.
The Preservation League of New York State has named the graving dock as one of seven endangered historic sites in the state.
Located at 1 Beard St., the basin is 710 feet long, 118 feet wide and 42 feet deep.
The suit contends that Ikea proposes to build a retaining wall within the graving dock, fill it on the landward side, pave over the fill and make that portion part of a parking lot. The remainder of the graving dock would be flooded again by opening the gate, and the new retaining wall would serve as a bulkhead. jmarzulli@nydailynews.com
